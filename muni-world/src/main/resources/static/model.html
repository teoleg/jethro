What the model concludes

Step 1 — the discount curve, step by step

The lattice divides the bond's life into . Each step's zero rate comes from the stated curve; the discount factor e−y·t is what $1 arriving at that time is worth today. Every valuation below is just cash flows × these discount factors, with the call decision layered on top.

Step 2 — the short-rate lattice (first years shown)

Each column is a coupon date; each box a possible short rate (½/½ up/down, spread σ√Δt apart in log terms — that is what "lognormal, σ = your input" means). The lattice is calibrated: discounting $1 through it reproduces the Step-1 curve exactly, so before any bond is priced the model already agrees with the market curve. The call is valued by walking this tree backwards: at every callable node the issuer redeems at the strike if that is cheaper for them than leaving the bond outstanding.

Step 3 — two values, and the option between them

Straight value prices the same cash flows with the call stripped. Callable value lets the issuer call optimally at every eligible node. The gap is the option value — what the issuer's right is costing you as the holder, in points. This is the book's central decomposition: callable = straight − option.

Step 4 — rate risk of the callable (effective, not textbook)

Computed by shifting the whole curve ±25bp and repricing on a recalibrated lattice at the same spread. For a bullet these approach the textbook duration/convexity; for a callable near its call, duration shortens and convexity goes negative — the price gains less when rates fall (the issuer calls) than it loses when rates rise. That asymmetry is the book's core warning about callable munis, and here it is measured, not asserted.
